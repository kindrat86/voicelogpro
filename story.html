The Builder Behind VoiceLogPro

The $47,000 Payment Dispute That Built VoiceLogPro

Every product starts with a wound. This one started on a jobsite — with a clipboard that got wet, a daily report that went missing, and a payment that never came.

The Day the Paperwork Failed

It was the end of a 14-hour shift. I had conduit in one hand and a clipboard in the other — the one with the daily report form the GC required. I wrote down the crew names, the materials delivered, the conversation about the change order. Then it rained. The clipboard got soaked. The ink ran. The form was illegible.

Two months later, that change order was disputed. The owner said the work wasn't documented. The GC asked for the daily report. I had nothing — just a memory of a rainy Tuesday and a ruined form. The dispute cost $47,000.

"I didn't lose because the work was bad. I lost because I couldn't prove we did it. The same hands that wired the building couldn't defend the invoice."

— The builder behind VoiceLogPro

That night, sitting in the truck with wet boots and a ruined clipboard, I had the thought that became VoiceLogPro: I can talk faster than I can type. Why can't I just speak the report — and have it formatted automatically?

I wasn't a software developer. I was an electrician who'd been burned by bad paperwork. But I knew exactly what the tool needed to do: record my voice, extract the key details, and produce a PDF that could stand up in court. Not a fancy app. Not a SaaS dashboard. Just a tool that worked while my hands were full.
