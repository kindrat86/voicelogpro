Three observed workflow risks

Each of the three below is an assumption drawn from the public pages above - not from inside information. They may be wrong; that is exactly what the pilot would test.

Assumption 1 - from public FAQ

Report writing is a second shift after the drive home

The FAQ states reports are typed at the office after the inspection, and that a report takes "a few hours to prepare." If that typing starts from scratch each time, every inspection day silently includes an unpaid desk evening. The bottleneck is not the inspection - it is the reconstruction of it.

Assumption 2 - from homepage + FAQ

One person is the ceiling on both inspection and reporting capacity

The homepage confirms Andy personally inspects every home; the FAQ confirms he also types every report. With "available 7 days a week" and a $300 base inspection, growth means adding desk hours to the same single person - capacity is capped by evenings, not by demand.

Assumption 3 - from public FAQ

Field detail has to survive the trip back to the office

The FAQ describes going back to research "items I wasn't sure of in the field" and looking up "specific appliance/furnace models and years." Anything not fully captured on-site - locations, model numbers, photo-to-finding links - must be remembered or re-found later. Unstructured notes lose their structure somewhere between the crawlspace and the keyboard.

What structured field logs change

VoiceLogPro turns voice memos into structured field logs: timestamped, organized entries spoken while you walk the property. For a solo inspector, the shift is this - the walk-through itself produces an organized record (location, observation, item, model numbers as you say them), so office time becomes reviewing and deepening a structured draft instead of typing one from a blank page.

To be precise about what this is not: VoiceLogPro does not write your inspection report and does not replace your judgment or your reporting software. It structures what you already say out loud, so the detail that currently lives in memory and margin notes arrives at the office already organized.
